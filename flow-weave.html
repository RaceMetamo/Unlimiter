Forces
No source · A video file drives motion exactly as the webcam does, and either can also be used as the dye.
Forces push the velocity field; the fluid integrates them over time, so motion input is smoothed by the simulation rather than shown raw. Threshold rejects the small global luminance shifts that webcam auto-exposure produces.
Fluid
Vorticity adds force proportional to curl, and curl grows with speed — unbounded, that feeds itself into noise. This caps both the confinement force and the velocity, so you can push vorticity and time step much harder before it breaks.
The velocity field is solved coarse and interpolated, as in the gpu-io example. Dye runs at full resolution, so detail comes from the dye, not the solver.
Spray & drips
Deposition is stochastic — that speckle at the edge of the cone is what separates spray from an airbrush gradient. Low hardness is a fat cap, high is a skinny one.
Run speed is capped at one cell per step — above that the transport amplifies instead of flowing. Paint runs only where more has built up than the wall can hold, so a run drains its source and tapers on its own. Solids act as ledges — drips pool on them rather than running off.
Heat
Off by default. Raise Brush heat or Motion heat to introduce heat, then Buoyancy to make it rise. Heat is advected like the dye. Weight pulls the hottest fluid back down, which is what makes a plume roll over into a mushroom rather than climbing forever. Negative brush heat draws cold, sinking fluid.
Obstacles
Solid regions the fluid cannot enter — no flow inside, zero pressure gradient at the faces, dye pushed aside. Feed in architectural photography and the building becomes the obstacle; use the webcam and your silhouette does.
Dye
Rotation, stretch and warp act on the coordinate; gain, bias and steps act on the value. Every generator goes through the same stage, so the controls behave the same whichever you pick. Warp pushes any pattern toward the organic.
Colour is injected into the fluid and carried by it. Nothing resets, so there is no crossfade and no pulse — the gradients come from advection.
Filaments
Particles follow the velocity field and take their colour from the dye beneath them, leaving additive trails — filaments rather than washes. Short lifetimes and long trails give the wispy look; long lifetimes give continuous streamlines.
Surface
Height from dye luminance, differentiated into a normal and lit — the FluidNinja surface trick, applied to a real fluid.
Drive — audio, MIDI, modulation
Shared drive layer: audio bands and onsets, MIDI clock and CC, clock-locked LFOs, routed to any parameter. Silence and a bypassed drive both give you exactly the values you dialled in.
Output
MP4 where supported, WebM otherwise.
